About

I am a master’s student at the School of Data Science, Fudan University, and received my B.S. in Mathematics from Zhejiang University. My research focuses on embodied AI: agents that perceive the physical world through vision, sound and language, reason about what they perceive, and act in it.

Before this, I worked on video-to-audio generation — models that watch a scene and produce what it should sound like, on the right frame and in the right place. That work appeared at NeurIPS, ICML and ICLR, and two of its questions carry over directly to embodied settings: how a model can reason about a scene before it commits to an output, and how to represent space rather than only time.

Research

The questions I am pursuing sit at three points of the loop an embodied agent closes with its environment.

  1. (a)
    Multimodal perception. Grounding an agent in what it sees and hears — including sound as evidence of events outside the field of view, and of where they are. Related earlier work: OmniAudio
  2. (b)
    Reasoning before acting. Structured, chain-of-thought decomposition of a scene as the step between perception and action, with feedback that addresses each part of that decomposition separately. Related earlier work: ThinkSound, PrismAudio
  3. (c)
    Representations for action. Compact, structured latent spaces of 3D scenes and their dynamics that a policy can plan and act in. Related earlier work: STAR-VAE
